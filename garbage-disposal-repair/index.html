Garbage Disposal Repair in Austin, TX

We repair humming, jammed, leaking, and backed-up kitchen disposals.

Call (737) 263-1705 Ask a question

In older Austin kitchens, the disposal often remains beneath the original sink while cabinets, countertops, and dishwasher hoses have changed around it. We see worn mounting rings, hardened drain gaskets, and jammed grinding chambers in homes across Austin, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills, and Lost Creek. Food scraps, grease, and a utensil dropped through the splash guard can stop the unit or leave water in the sink.

We start by separating a jam inside the grinding chamber from a drain blockage, a failed switch, or a leak at the sink flange. A humming disposal can usually wait with its switch off and the sink unused. Water pooling in the cabinet or backing into the sink should not wait, because it can soak cabinet panels and run through the dishwasher connection.

Humming disposal and sink water

Customers often call because the disposal hums but will not spin, makes a grinding sound, or stays completely silent when the wall switch is turned on. Others report sink water that rises when the dishwasher drains, or food scraps that remain in the basin after the unit runs. A reset button that will not hold can point to a jammed motor, an overloaded circuit, or a worn disposal.

A sour odor from the sink may come from residue inside the grinding chamber, while water beneath the unit can come from the discharge elbow, dishwasher inlet, sink flange, or disposal housing. We check the source before replacing parts or changing the drain connection. Similar sounds and puddles can come from very different components, so the repair work and parts required can vary once we inspect the cabinet.

Under-sink inspection

We first isolate power at the switch or circuit, then check the reset button, outlet, cord, mounting ring, splash guard, and grinding chamber. We inspect the discharge elbow, P-trap, dishwasher hose, and sink flange for loosened connections or water marks. A disposal wrench can free some jams without removing the unit, while a damaged turntable or cracked housing requires a different repair.

We use a meter to verify power at the outlet instead of assuming the disposal motor has failed, and we check the alignment of the drain connection with the typical 1 1/2-inch sink drain. A run, leak, and drain test commonly takes 20 to 40 minutes, depending on cabinet access and the fault. We can often clear a simple jam, replace a gasket, or fit an available replacement unit during the visit; a cracked flange, altered drain route, or model that must be ordered is booked for follow-up work.

Mounting ring and cabinet access

The disposal's age, model, and mounting ring affect the parts needed for the repair. A corroded mounting assembly, a seized drain nut, or a leaking sink flange can add removal work beyond the motor or grinding chamber. Tight cabinet walls, stored items, and a dishwasher hose routed across the unit also change the access beneath the sink.

A repair stays more straightforward when the existing drain elbow, power cord, and dishwasher inlet match the replacement part. Work expands if the selected disposal needs a different mounting adapter, a new drain connection, or an ordered component that does not match the old unit. We inspect those connections before setting out the work needed at the sink cabinet.

Model plate and sink cabinet

Before calling, check the model number and make on the plate underneath or along the side of the disposal body. Let us know whether the unit hums, leaks, trips the reset button, or leaves water standing in the sink. If you know when the disposal was installed, that can help us identify compatible mounting and drain parts.

Please tell us which sink cabinet holds the unit, especially if the kitchen has a bar sink or prep sink as well. Clear cleaners, bins, and stored pans from the cabinet if possible so we can reach the mounting ring and drain elbow.
