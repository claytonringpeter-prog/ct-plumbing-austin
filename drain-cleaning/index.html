Drain Cleaning in Austin, TX

We clear slow drains, backups, gurgling sinks, and sewer odors in Austin.

Call (737) 263-1705 Ask a question

A sink that gurgles after the dishwasher drains, a shower pan that holds water, or a sewer odor near a floor drain can point to buildup inside a branch line. Water may still reach the pipe, but grease, soap, hair, paper, or roots can reduce the opening until the fixture drains slowly or sends water back toward the room. We trace the backup from the affected fixture toward the cleanout so we can tell whether the blockage sits in a trap, a branch pipe, or a longer drain run.

A single slow basin without standing water can sometimes wait for a scheduled visit, especially if no other fixture backs up. A toilet that rises, a tub that fills during laundry, or wastewater near a floor drain should not wait, because a blocked drain line can overflow onto flooring, cabinets, and wall materials. We clear the obstruction before repeated use pushes more water against the blockage.

Gurgling sinks and sewer odors

We hear about kitchen sinks that drain slowly after a dishwasher cycle, bathroom sinks that bubble when the toilet flushes, and showers that leave a puddle around the drain cover. We also hear about a sewer smell from a utility room, a washing machine standpipe that spills, or a toilet bowl that rises before it empties. Each sound, smell, and backup gives us a starting point, but none identifies the exact section of pipe by itself.

We check whether water returns through another fixture, whether the trap holds water, and whether the blockage affects one room or several drains. A greasy kitchen branch, a hair-packed shower line, and a root-filled exterior pipe require different tools and different amounts of access. Similar symptoms can hide very different drain faults, so the work involved and the charge can change once we locate the obstruction.

Drain line inspection

We begin at the fixture and accessible cleanout, checking the trap, drain opening, pipe diameter, and direction of the backup. Most sink and shower branches use pipe from 1 1/2 inches to 2 inches across, while a toilet drain commonly connects to a larger pipe. We note how far a cable travels, whether it meets solid resistance, and whether water clears after the line opens.

A basic fixture and cleanout check often takes 10 to 20 minutes, while a cable run or camera inspection takes longer when the blockage sits farther along the pipe. We can often clear an accessible branch line during the same visit, then flush water through the fixture and inspect the flow. We replace a trap washer or cleanout plug only if removal shows that the existing piece will not reseal, while a collapsed pipe, separated joint, or buried line repair needs a separate scheduled scope.

Pipe access and blockage depth

Pipe material and pipe age affect the work because older cast iron can collect scale along the interior wall, while PVC often has smoother pipe walls but can still hold grease or debris at a fitting. A blockage near a sink trap usually gives us a shorter route than buildup farther down a branch or main drain. Roots, hardened grease, and construction debris can also require more clearing passes than loose hair or soap residue.

Access changes the scope as well. An exterior cleanout near the foundation gives us a direct entry point, while a line behind a vanity, beneath a crawl space, or through a roof vent can take more setup and protection for the surrounding room. Camera findings that show a broken pipe, a shifted joint, or a buried obstruction may call for further line work beyond clearing the drain opening.

Fixture and cleanout location

Before you call, note which room and fixture back up first, such as the kitchen sink, hall bathroom tub, laundry standpipe, or floor drain. Tell us whether water stands in the basin, returns through another drain, or continues to run from a dishwasher or washing machine connection. If a garbage disposal sits under the kitchen sink, let us know whether the backup began while it was running.

Look for an exterior cleanout cap near the foundation or yard and tell us if furniture, stored boxes, or a locked gate blocks access to it. A photo of the drain opening, trap, or cleanout area can help us plan the cable route and protect nearby cabinets or flooring.
