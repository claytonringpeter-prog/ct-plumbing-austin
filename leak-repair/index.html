Leak Repair in Austin, TX

We trace active leaks in Austin homes, from wet cabinets to water-stained drywall.

Call (737) 263-1705 Ask a question

A drip below the kitchen sink can darken the cabinet floor within days, then soften particleboard around the supply valves. After a week, water often tracks along the drain opening, cabinet seam, or wall plate, so the damp spot extends beyond the original fitting. We follow the wet path before it reaches drywall, flooring, or the room below.

A pinhole in a copper pipe may start with only a faint hiss, yet a month of moisture can stain a ceiling, swell a baseboard, and feed mold behind a wall. Seasonal movement and repeated pressure cycles can also turn a loose compression nut into a steady leak under a vanity or behind a refrigerator line. Leak repair should not wait because water keeps moving through wood, drywall, insulation, and wall cavities after the visible drip looks small.

Dripping faucets and wet cabinets

Customers call us about a cabinet that is wet under the sink, a faucet that drips after the handle is closed, or a puddle that returns after they wipe it up. We also hear about a ceiling spot below a bathroom, a water meter that moves while every fixture is off, and a hissing sound behind drywall. A damp ring around a shutoff valve or a rusty supply tube can point to a different leak than water collecting around a drain trap.

A valve stem can leak only while a handle turns, while a cracked supply line can release water continuously under pressure. A drain joint may stay dry until a sink or shower runs, whereas a pipe in a wall can leave no visible water at the fixture at all. These leaks can produce similar stains and puddles, but the failed part, access route, and repair work differ, so they do not carry the same cost.

Meter test and pipe inspection

We start by locating the wet fixture, checking the cabinet, wall, ceiling, supply connections, shutoff valves, and nearby pipe route. With fixtures off, we watch the water meter for movement and use a pressure gauge to compare the static reading with the reading while water runs. Residential water pressure commonly falls between 40 and 80 psi, and an unusually high reading can keep stressing a weak valve, connector, or pipe joint.

A visual inspection with meter and pressure checks often takes 20 to 40 minutes, though a concealed leak can require additional tracing through a wall, ceiling, or crawlspace. We can usually replace an accessible supply tube, compression fitting, angle stop, washer, or section of exposed pipe during the same visit after isolating the water line. Pipe routing behind tile, inside a cabinet back, beneath flooring, or through a slab may need a separate visit so the opening, replacement material, and any wall repair can be planned correctly.

Shutoff valves and wall access

The age and material of the failed fitting affect the work involved. An exposed PEX supply tube under a vanity gives us a direct route to the connection, while aging galvanized pipe, corroded copper, or a brittle plastic fitting may require us to remove more material before we can make a sound repair. Water damage can also weaken a cabinet floor or drywall panel around the pipe, adding access work before the new fitting goes in.

A seized shutoff valve can prevent us from isolating one fixture cleanly, which may require work at another valve or at the main water line. An exact cartridge, specialty valve, or matching connector may need to be ordered if the existing part uses a less common size or manufacturer-specific fitting. If rerouting pipe, opening a wall, or altering a plumbing run calls for a permit or sign-off, that scope adds coordination beyond an accessible connection repair.

Fixture model numbers and access

Before calling, note which room has the leak and which fixture sits above or beside the wet area. The make and model code may appear on a faucet body, trim plate, valve body, or installation paperwork, and a photo of any lettering can help us match a cartridge, supply tube, or connector. Let us know the approximate age of the fitting and whether water runs only while the fixture is on or continues after it is shut off.

Please also describe whether the leak sits inside a vanity, behind drywall, below a ceiling, or in a crawlspace, and whether a cabinet or access panel blocks the pipe. If you can safely see the water source, tell us whether it comes from a handle, shutoff valve, supply line, drain joint, or pipe connection.
